Climate Risk Report · Q2 2026 Operating Review

Five risks. Four companies. $8.9M a year.

Northwood Capital's ten portfolio companies generate $1.7 billion of revenue and 23,032 metric tons of direct emissions. The risks attached to those emissions — transitional, physical, regulatory — sum to $8.9 million per year of unhedged exposure, concentrated in just five issues across four portfolio companies: Keystone, Harvest Ridge, NovaCare, and Sterling.

None of them are existential. All of them are addressable inside the current hold period. What follows is a guided tour of what they are, what we'd do, and what we still don't know — twelve entries, organized into four sections. Pick any.

Deep Dive 01 · Sterling Precision Manufacturing

The slow-motion tax that hits at exit

Risk: CBAM phase-inProbability: 90%$/yr at full phase: $524,362Quadrant: Mitigate Now

The European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism is not a question of whether — it is a question of when, and at what rate. The mechanism phases in linearly between 2026 and 2034, applying a price (denominated in EU ETS allowances) to the embedded carbon of imported steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, and a handful of other goods.

Sterling Precision Manufacturing exports approximately 30% of its output to EU customers — that figure is the most consequential unverified number in this report, which is why the chart below shows it as a range, not a point. At a 30% share, the CBAM cost in 2030 is $244,975. At full phase-in in 2034, it reaches $524,362 — roughly 1.74% of EBITDA in that year.

Sensitivity to EU export share

Five-year hold-period CBAM cost at five EU-export-share assumptions. The 30% case is base. Each step up or down changes the hold-period cost by roughly $168,000.
Assumption — Critical

Sterling's EU export share is a firm estimate. We have it from Sterling's commercial deck, not from a verified shipping ledger. A range of 10–50% is plausible; the model output swings 5×.

Cost trajectory across phase-in

Year-by-year CBAM cost at three EU-share assumptions. The shape is the same — quadratic-ish — because CBAM phases in linearly while EU ETS prices rise. The level is what shifts.
Recommended action

Commission a 30-day product-by-product carbon footprint study at Sterling. Cost: low five figures, scoped against three vendors. Output: a verified bill-of-materials in CO₂ terms, which collapses the model uncertainty and creates a basis for procurement substitution.

Deep Dive 02 · Keystone Behavioral Health

A $5.9 million haircut waiting at the closing table

Risk: ESG exit multiple discountProbability: 55%Expected EV impact: -$5.9MQuadrant: Mitigate Now

Keystone is the single largest dollar exposure in this report, and it is structurally different from every other risk: it is a one-time event at exit, not an annual cash-flow leak. It belongs in the report because the magnitude makes it impossible to ignore — but the framing matters. We are not modelling foregone EBITDA; we are modelling the buyer's willingness to pay.

The current portfolio company carbon intensity at Keystone is 7.3 mt/$M revenue. A reasonable target — informed by other healthcare-services platforms in our pipeline review, though not from a published benchmark we can cite — is roughly 5.0 mt/$M. The gap is small in absolute terms (about 317 mt/yr at this revenue base), but the multiple-impact at exit is sensitive to which buyer the deal team takes the company to.

Buyer-type scenarios

From base-case enterprise value to probability-weighted EV. Each bar is one buyer scenario weighted by its probability. The ESG-First Buyer case is small in probability (5%) but large in impact (-1.0× multiple).
The math is asymmetric

Probability-weighted EV impact: -$5.9M. Cost to close the intensity gap: $44,436 (LED retrofits, HVAC controls, two facility audits). Implied ROI on the gap-closure work: 134×.

This calculation rests on probabilities the deal team supplied verbally; we have not validated them against transaction data. The Keystone exit timeline is roughly 24–30 months out, which means the gap-closure capex must be deployed in the next two quarters to have time to produce two clean reporting periods before the auction begins.

Recommended action

Approve the $44,436 efficiency package this quarter. Treat it as a valuation hedge, not an operating initiative — the IRR framing understates its purpose.

Deep Dive 03 · Harvest Ridge Foods

We were wrong about Murfreesboro by a factor of two and a half

Source: WRI Aqueduct 4.0Method: CARTO SQL APIStatus: Hard Data

The original Harvest Ridge water analysis used simulated stress indices for each of the five facilities — values seeded from regional averages. The simulated number for HRF-002 in Murfreesboro, Tennessee was 1.4, classified as Low-Medium. The actual value, fetched from the World Resources Institute's Aqueduct 4.0 dataset using a PostGIS spatial join against the HydroSHEDS basin layer, is 3.51 — High.

The Stones River sub-basin, which serves Murfreesboro, has a withdrawal-to-supply ratio of 40–80%. This is consistent with the city's documented periodic curtailment events. The simulation got it wrong because regional averages smoothed over a sub-basin that is much more stressed than its surroundings.

Five facilities, simulated vs actual

Each line is a Harvest Ridge facility. Four of the five tracked close to the simulation. Murfreesboro did not. The +2.11 delta is the largest data-quality correction in the report.
The finding that changed the plan

This single API call added a new "Mitigate Now"–adjacent risk worth $850,000/yr in expected value, and prompted a new $220,000 capex initiative (Murfreesboro water resilience). Both are now in the recommended plan.

Deep Dive 04 · Materiality

Fifteen risks, four quadrants, one priority list

Total risks: 15Mitigate Now: 5$ at risk (top quadrant): $8.9M/yrCategories: 5

The materiality matrix is the single chart we'd put in front of a Managing Partner if she had thirty seconds. It plots every named risk in the portfolio on two axes — probability of occurrence (horizontal) and annual dollar impact (vertical, log scale) — and uses the four quadrants to suggest a default response.

Five risks land in the upper-right "Mitigate Now" quadrant — high probability and high impact. They sum to $8.9M/yr and represent the focus of the rest of this report. Three more risks are in "Hedge / Insure" — low probability but very high impact, where the right tool is insurance or contractual transfer rather than direct mitigation.

Each dot is one named risk. Size encodes dollar impact. Whiskers show our probability range — wider whiskers mean less confidence in the central estimate. Colors encode risk category (transition, physical, social, governance, regulatory).
How to read this

Top-right quadrant: act now. Top-left: hedge or insure. Bottom-right: monitor and revisit quarterly. Bottom-left: deprioritize until something changes. The quadrant lines are at 30% probability and $500,000/yr impact — drawn for editorial clarity, not regulatory definition.

Deep Dive 05 · NovaCare Health Partners

The only Mitigate-Now risk we can't directly price

Risk: Clinical staff shortageProbability: 65%$/yr: $720,000Quadrant: Mitigate Now

Four of the five risks in our top quadrant have a clean financial mechanism: regulatory fee, exit multiple, customer requirement, asset damage. NovaCare's clinical staffing risk is the exception. The number — $720,000/yr — is a planning estimate based on overtime premiums, agency-staff markups, and the lost-margin cost of unfilled shifts at NovaCare's 30 facilities. It is not a contractual cash flow.

This matters for two reasons. First, the lever is not directly ours. Hiring decisions, retention programs, and clinical compensation belong to NovaCare's operating team and HR function. Second, the climate connection is indirect: increasing summer heat and humidity in NovaCare's southeastern footprint plausibly correlates with both higher patient volume and elevated staff burnout — a relationship we have inferred from anecdotal portco reporting, not from a primary dataset, and which we would want to validate before acting on.

Deep Dive 06 · Portfolio-wide Scope 3

Their carbon problem is becoming our customers' carbon problem

Method: EPA USEEIO 2.0 spend-basedTotal Scope 3: 297,120 mtS3 / S1+2 ratio: 12.9×Status: Benchmark

Scope 3 — emissions from a portfolio company's supply chain, not its own facilities — is, in aggregate, 12.9 times larger than the directly-measured Scope 1 and 2 footprint. We did not measure these numbers; we estimated them using EPA's USEEIO model, which assigns sector-level emissions intensities (mt CO₂e per $M revenue) to seven NAICS-mapped portco categories.

The estimate matters because at least one portfolio company — Harvest Ridge Foods — has a $34M annual contract with a national grocery customer that has signalled it will require Scope 3 disclosure from suppliers starting in 2027. That is a 60% probability event with $680,000/yr in expected value (lost margin if the contract is renegotiated downward).

Where the supply-chain emissions live

Each rectangle is one portfolio company, sized by estimated Scope 3 emissions. Harvest Ridge dominates because food and agriculture have the highest USEEIO factor (420 mt/$M). Sterling is second because of steel inputs.
Methodological honesty

USEEIO factors are sector averages, not supplier-specific measurements. Real Scope 3 for a given portco can vary 10–50× from the sector average depending on supplier mix. These numbers are useful for LP conversations and order-of-magnitude planning. They are not useful for compliance or contractual disclosure.

Deep Dive 07 · The recommended plan

$3.3 million of capex against $779,617 of NPV

Initiatives: 10Total capex: $3.27MTotal NPV: $779,617Discount rate: 10%

The ten operational initiatives are ranked by net present value over a five-year hold at a 10% discount rate. Seven are NPV-positive on a strict cash-flow basis. Three are not — and they earn their place in the plan because they protect valuation at exit, not because they generate cash during the hold.

Two of the seven positive-NPV initiatives are flagged Critical: Harvest Ridge flood mitigation and Harvest Ridge water recycling at Bowling Green. Both reflect physical-risk exposure compounding faster than insurance escalation can absorb. The first kicks off this quarter (Q2 2026); the second follows in Q3.

Capex versus NPV, ranked

Each row is one initiative. Gray bar = capex (left scale). Marker = NPV (right scale, right of the zero line is positive). Initiatives sorted by NPV descending.
The seven we'd approve this quarter

Harvest Ridge flood ($396K NPV); Sterling renewable PPA ($271K, zero capex); Harvest Ridge CIP recycling ($116K); Murfreesboro resilience ($105K); Trident cold-aisle ($79K); Harvest Ridge steam recovery ($52K); Pinnacle resilience assessment ($50K). Total NPV: $1.07M. Total capex: $1.30M.

The three that need a separate conversation

Sterling process-heat electrification (-$3K NPV, but the cleanest demonstrable decarbonization story for an exit narrative); Harvest Ridge backup water at Bowling Green (-$136K NPV, but insurance against a $9M curtailment tail); Clearview fleet pilot (-$150K NPV, but signals to LPs and primes the larger fleet program). Approve as strategic capex with explicit non-cash justification.

Deep Dive 08 · New initiative — Q3 2026

A new initiative born from a single API call

Capex: $220,000Annual benefit: $78,000NPV: $105,250Start: Q3 2026

This initiative did not exist in version 1, version 2, or version 3 of this analysis. It was added in version 4, after a single HTTP request to the WRI Aqueduct 4.0 dataset returned a water-stress score for HRF-002 Murfreesboro that was 2.5× higher than the simulated value we had been working from.

The recommended package — total $220K capex — combines three workstreams in indicative proportions: rainwater harvesting for non-potable use, a closed-loop cooling retrofit on the primary processing line, and a 12-month metering and analytics program to baseline actual consumption. The line-item split will be finalized once the facility audit is complete in Q2. The framing assumption is that Tennessee's withdrawal-permit framework tightens before exit; we do not yet have a probability we are willing to defend on that timeline.

The payback comparison is tighter than it appears on the surface. Without this initiative, the Murfreesboro facility carries an expected-value loss of $850,000/yr from the new water-stress risk. The $220K capex therefore protects roughly 4× its cost in addition to the cash savings — a calculation we deliberately did not embed in the NPV figure to keep the financial math conservative.

What this entry says about our process

The simulated value (1.4) was not unreasonable. It was based on regional averages. It just happened to be wrong for this specific sub-basin. The lesson is not that simulations are bad — it is that real data, where available, is cheap insurance against expensive surprises.

Deep Dive 09 · Sequencing

Six quarters to get the critical work done

Span: Q2 2026 → Q3 2027Critical: 2 initiativesHigh: 4 initiativesMedium: 4 initiatives

The sequencing is not arbitrary. Two constraints drive it: capex availability (no portco can absorb more than two simultaneous projects), and the dependency between baseline-establishing assessments (Pinnacle climate resilience, Harvest Ridge water metering) and the capex projects they inform.

The two Critical initiatives — Harvest Ridge flood mitigation, Harvest Ridge water recycling at Bowling Green — both kick off in Q2 and Q3 of 2026 respectively. Process-heat electrification at Sterling is the longest project (six quarters) and starts in Q1 2027 to give the bill-of-materials work time to land first.

What this proves about the methodology

Three things. One: the framework was robust enough that better data slotted in without rebuilding. Two: the data-quality classification system flagged the simulated values as such, so no one was surprised when one of them was wrong. Three: the next investment in this analysis — closing the two Critical Sterling gaps — will produce a similar-magnitude improvement in CBAM accuracy.
